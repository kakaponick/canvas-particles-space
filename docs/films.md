# Films

The rules for the code that makes films. `CLAUDE.md` holds the words *gated* and *deterministic* and the invariants of the scene a film draws, `docs/scene.md` the laws of its parts. Fractal films reuse the readers, captions, caption text and script engine described here, and a reel plays fractal clips; their own rules are in `docs/fractal_films.md`. Read the section of the code you change:

- The film and its plan: `src/lib/film.ts`, `agent_values.ts`, `intro_tracks.ts`, the scene's `fire`, `makeTitle` and `useTitle`.
- The film's scene: `film_render.ts`, `film_live.ts`, `film_session.ts`, `film_mode.tsx`, `film_library.ts`.
- Scripts: `film_script.ts`.
- Reels: `reel.ts`, `reel_render.ts`.
- Over the frame (fade, shapes, captions): `film_graphics.ts`, `film_captions.ts`, `caption_text.ts`, `public/fonts`, `index.css`.
- Encoding and output: `encodeVideo`, `encodeLocally`, `frame_pack.ts`, `mcp/local_*.cjs`.
- The `space` MCP server: `mcp/space_mcp.mjs`.
- Narration: `scripts/voice.mjs`, `scripts/drama3_*`.
- Verify: the checks a change owes.

## The film and its plan

A film is the scene as a 1-180 s mp4, described by one JSON object: frame and length, seed, starting settings, the intro as a clip, keyframes for any numeric setting or intro parameter, captions. Its format is written once, as `FILM_GUIDE` in `src/lib/film.ts`, and the script language as `SCRIPT_GUIDE` in `src/lib/film_script.ts`; `window.space.film.guide()` and the `space_guide` tool return both, so the guide changes with the format or the language.

- **`planFilm` checks a film against the same registries as `window.space`** (`src/lib/agent_values.ts`), so a simulation setting or intro parameter is settable and animatable in films once it has its spec (plus the two lines `CLAUDE.md` names for one that is neither a slider nor a colour). On a track a switch flips at its key's second (`TRACK_SWITCHES`); a colour (`COLORS`) is keyed as `'#rrggbb'`, moves channel by channel as numbers like the opening's colours, and `applyTracks` writes it back as the text the store keeps; the palette is set, never moved. Each part is read through `attempt`, which keeps the problem and reads on, so a film is told all its mistakes at once. What a film asks for and will not get is a note, not silence (the opening's film look without `filmHold`, a face without the words' letters).
- **A film is a timeline, each ability of the scene one kind of thing on it**: a value moves along keyframes (`tracks`), a layer switches (`layers`), the intro is a clip (`intro`), an intro event fires again (`cues`), the scene's own time slows or stops (`speed`); the audit holds that a film does what the intro debugger does with time and layers. `shots` name stretches that change no frame and make a preview a storyboard, one frame from the middle of each. `planFilm` returns the film as it understood it (`timeline`), which `check` and every preview show.
- **A layer is declared once in `src/lib/intro_tracks.ts`**: a line in `INTRO_LAYER_NOTES`, and a place in `CUE_LAYERS` if it is an event; `describe`, the guide and the checks read those.
- **`speed` bends the scene's step, never the film's seconds**: the intro's start is worked out from it in scene time (`introTime`), and slow motion takes finer steps.
- **A fired event is drawn by the opening's own code.** `scene.fire` plays events on three clocks kept with the scene (the blast, the ringing, a title), sampling the intro's timeline into a second frame laid over the first, so it is gated by whether one plays. A fired title's words are drawn before the first frame (`scene.makeTitle`) and put in place at the cue (`scene.useTitle`), since a film cannot wait for a face midway. What they are drawn from (size, widest line, line spacing, weight, tracking, the halo's reach and streak: `TITLE_DRAWN_KEYS` in `intro_params.ts`) is read then only, by `titleStyle` in `film_render.ts` and `readTitleStyle` on the page: a new parameter of the title's sprite goes into the list and both readers, and `planFilm` tells a film with a track on one that it moves nothing.
- **The opening's film look** (letterbox, vignette, grain, fringe) leaves with the opening, but for the share the intro parameter `filmHold` keeps.

## The film's scene

- **A film plays in a scene of its own** (`src/lib/film_render.ts`): a second `createScene` on a canvas of the film's size, started from the page's defaults (never this browser's settings) and stepped by a fixed `dt` with no clock, so the page's scene and saved settings are never touched and a slow machine draws a fast one's frames. Up to 60 fps, motion steps at about 60 Hz (`frame.present` false on the steps between two frames); above that once a frame, up to 240 fps: the same motion at any rate, not the same frames. Tracks move at every step, each at its own second, so a fast one (a turning camera) streaks as it does live. A preview frame is the frame the render makes.
- **Its particles are stepped by the graphics card as the page's are** (`src/lib/field_step.ts`, the rule in `CLAUDE.md`), so a film needs no workers for them and no header of the server, and its bytes are *deterministic*: the bytes of two builds are compared on one machine.
- **The film mode plays a film live** (`src/lib/film_live.ts`, driven by `film_session.ts` and `film_mode.tsx`) with the players a render draws with (`createFilmPlayer`, `createFractalPlayer`, `createReelPlayer`), at the screen's size, paced by the clock, so every frame shown is the render's. A frame there is no time for is stepped unshown; a film further behind slows down rather than skip ahead. A scene only goes forward, so playing again from the end makes a new player. A film loaded there goes through the same planners and `renderFilm`, `renderFractal`, `renderReel`, and finds the files it names (a reel's clips, a shape's picture) among those given beside it, then in `films/`, which `film_library.ts` bundles as lazy chunks. A new kind of film plays live once `loadFilm` and `makePlayer` know it.

## Scripts

- **A script is the film's JSON, said shorter** (`src/lib/film_script.ts`): JavaScript that plays the film forward on a cursor of time (`wait`, `to`, `drive`, `shot`, `caption`, `fire`, `meanwhile`), so no second is written by hand, a part of a film is a function, and the film lasts as long as what was played. `writeFilm` runs it in the page and returns the object `planFilm` takes; `check`, `preview` and `render` take a string as a script. Only that JSON reaches the renderer, so a new ability enters the film format first, then gets its word in `SCRIPT_WORDS`, each word told in `SCRIPT_GUIDE`.
- **A value's moves become keys when the script ends** (`writeKeys`): a film's own ease is one key, a spring or a function 60 linear keys a second (`BAKE_RATE`); two moves of one value at once are refused, naming both lines. A script's chance, `Math.random` included, comes from the film's seed, so the same script is the same film.
- **A mistake names its line**, found on the stack through the script's `sourceURL`; a grammar mistake gets its line in the MCP server (`vm.Script`, which reads and never runs), since the page's engine does not give it.
- **The audit holds** that the guide tells every word and its example runs, that a script and its JSON give the same plan, that a script moves everything a film moves, and that the lines are named.

## Reels

A reel is several films in one video (`src/lib/reel.ts`, `src/lib/reel_render.ts`): a description with `clips`, taken by the same commands and tools as a film.

- **A reel draws nothing itself.** A third player beside the film's and the fractal's, it has the clip's own player draw each frame and copies it to one canvas (two players in a dissolve, the second laid over by its share; black is a fill), which goes to the same `encodeVideo`. A clip draws in a reel the frames it draws alone, with the same seed and steps.
- **A kind of clip** is one entry in `CLIP_READERS` (read and plan) and one in `CLIP_PLAYERS` (make its player); the audit fails until it has a film to plan.
- **A player is made when its first frame is near and disposed after its last**, so only a dissolve holds two scenes; one that cannot get its second scene plays through black with a note. A clip given as a path is read by the film server, never by the page.

## Over the frame: fade, shapes and captions

Over the finished frame lie, from below: `fade`, a share of black (`filmFade`, drawn in `film_render.ts`), the shapes, the captions. Shapes and captions share one 2D canvas, never the scene; the page has no captions.

- **Shapes** (`graphics`, `src/lib/film_graphics.ts`): a kind is declared once in `film.ts` (`GRAPHIC_KINDS`, its keys in `GRAPHIC_KEYS`, read by `readGraphics`); the audit holds that each plans with every key and that the guide tells it. Pictures are decoded before the first frame; the film server hands the page a path on its machine under a one-off token, as it does a sound file. A film without shapes never calls into that module.
- **A place on the scene** (`{ hole: [dx, dy] }`, in a shape or as a caption's `at`) goes on the frame through `holeOnFrame` and `placeOnFrame` in `film_captions.ts`, the one home of that sum, which reads the camera from the frame's composite settings; a number given by name is read as a caption's `#{key}` is.
- **What crowds the words is measured as the frames are drawn** (`FrameCrowd` in `film_captions.ts`): each shape hands the crowd the strokes it drew on a frame, each caption checks its block against those and the blocks drawn before it, and a meeting is kept once with its first and last second. The preview, the render and `review.mjs sheets` print them as `Crowded:` lines and the manifest carries them for the reviewer, so the machine finds a label run through by a path or laid over another before an eye must. A new thing drawn over the frame in strokes hands them to the crowd as the shapes do; a film without captions measures nothing.
- **Captions** (`src/lib/film_captions.ts`): a line wider than the frame breaks at its spaces (`fitCaption`, worked out once per caption); words in a face that lacks their letters go to the bundled face that has them (`faceFor` in `film.ts`: of the display faces only Unbounded has Cyrillic), because a fallback to the machine's own face would make the same film a different video elsewhere.
- **A caption's text is written as popular science is** (`src/lib/caption_text.ts`, read once by `planFilm`): a mark is a sign with a brace right after it, so plain text's own underscores and asterisks are never marks (`_{s}` lowered, `^{2}` raised, `*{term}` in the accent colour, `#{key:1}` a number read from the film at that second: the caption's own `values`, the film's `seconds`, or any number of the scene after the tracks).
- **The fonts**: behind every bundled face stand Noto Sans, Noto Sans Math and Noto Sans Symbols (Greek, mathematics, the signs of the Sun and the planets), in `public/fonts`, each declared in `index.css` with only the characters the faces before it lack, so a caption loads no file it will not use and the page none at all. `COVERED` in `caption_text.ts` is every character those files hold, read from their character maps; the note about the machine's own face goes by it, so a new font file means a new `COVERED`.
- **The layout**: a caption of one line without marks is drawn as one string; a line with marks, a caption that arrives a letter, a word or a line at a time (`reveal`), and one with `align` are drawn piece by piece from one layout (`layOut`). A caption with a live number is laid out again when its words change and keeps the block of its widest number (`steady`), so nothing shakes as digits change.

## Encoding and output

- **A film is encoded by WebCodecs** through `mediabunny`, imported only when a film is rendered, so visitors never download it: the encoder of every page, and of the film server on a machine without the one below. The browser uses the graphics card only for a frame size and rate it offers the card's encoder at together (here 1080p up to 60 a second, 4K up to 30), and software past that, a third as fast. A film is not encoded in real time, so `hardwareRate` finds a rate the card's encoder takes the frame at and the encoder is told that one; the frames carry their own times, so 4K at 60 fps or 1080p at 240 is encoded on the card (88 frames a second at 4K against 35; RTX 3070). The card's encoder is driven by a quantizer per quality (`QUALITY_QUANTIZER`), since told a bitrate it holds one picture whatever the budget; the software encoder keeps the bitrate, counted for 60 fps at most (`BITRATE_FPS`). A stream above the level the browser's encoder names gets the true one written into the decoder's description (`raiseAvcLevel`). What was measured and found of no use for speed is above `encodeVideo`.
- **The film server encodes outside the browser where the machine lets it**, faster at 1080p: 285 frames a second against the 205 of the browser's encoder on the card, and 77 against 88 at 4K (RTX 3070). A page in Chrome cannot hand frames to another program, so the server's browser is then Electron (`mcp/local_host.cjs`), which gives its page `window.spaceEncoder` (`mcp/local_encoder.cjs`): ffmpeg on `h264_nvenc`, taking NV12 frames over a socket of the machine. `encodeLocally` in `film_render.ts` feeds it: `src/lib/frame_pack.ts` turns the finished frame into NV12 on the card and reads it back a few frames behind the drawing. It replaces `encodeVideo`'s own encoder and nothing else, so every kind of film goes through it.
- **The machine is set up by hand, once**: Electron's program in `~/.space-film/electron/` and an ffmpeg with `h264_nvenc` at `~/.space-film/ffmpeg` (or on the `PATH`), or the two named by `SPACE_ELECTRON` and `SPACE_FFMPEG`. `findLocalEncoder` in the server looks, tries one frame through the card's encoder, and without either leaves the film to Chrome; `SPACE_BROWSER_ENCODER=1` asks for Chrome on a machine that has both. A render's answer names the encoder that made the file (`encoder`).
- **The two give the same frames, not the same file**: ffmpeg is given the bitrate a quality names and spends it, the browser's encoder on the card is given a quantizer and spends what the picture takes (at high, 1080p at 60 fps: 31 Mbit/s and 45), and ffmpeg writes the video range where Chrome writes the full one; both say so in the stream. In Electron the browser's own encoder is the slower at 4K as well, so there ffmpeg takes every frame; in Chrome the browser's encoder takes 4K a seventh faster than ffmpeg (88 frames a second against 77), which is what `SPACE_BROWSER_ENCODER=1` gets.
- **A render through the MCP server is never held in the page.** The file is sent as it is encoded, a piece at a time with the place it belongs at (`upload`, `at`), and the server writes each piece in place; room for the index is kept at the file's start (`fastStart: 'reserve'`, which needs the count of packets of every track), so the file plays before it is all there and its size is not bound by the tab's memory. The encoder outside the browser writes the file itself, at the path the server names (`local`). Only a film saved by hand with `download` is put together in the page.

## The `space` MCP server

`mcp/space_mcp.mjs` (in `.mcp.json`) is how an agent makes a film: `space_guide`, `space_describe`, `space_check` (the film read back as a timeline, without drawing), `space_preview` (a contact sheet of frames, the storyboard of a film cut into shots; with `still`, one frame at the film's own size with no label, a poster to publish, `stillSecond` in `film_render.ts`) and `space_render` (the mp4, with an optional audio file under it). Each takes a film as JSON (`film`) or as a script (`script`, with `input`). The page does the work through `window.space.film` (`check`, `preview`, `render`, `progress`, `cancel`), which also works from the console of any page with `?agent`.

- **It serves this machine's `dist/` and keeps one browser on it** (headless Chrome, or Electron where it encodes outside the browser), so run `pnpm build` after changing the page. It has no dependencies: JSON-RPC lines on stdio, the browser over its DevTools socket.
- **A preview and a render take the machine's turn** with the checks of `scripts/bench.mjs` (`takeTurn` in `scripts/turn.mjs`, the rule in `CLAUDE.md`); the tools that only read the page answer at once, and a new tool that draws takes the turn too (`DRAWS` in the server). The server starts its browser and loads the page before it takes the turn (`getReady`); a measurement takes its turn `alone` and waits for the browsers starting then. A wait is told when it begins and when it ends, as the call's progress to a client that follows it and on stderr to one that does not (`space.mjs`).
- **The shaders a browser compiled are kept for the next one** (`layShaders` and `keepShaders` in the server). On a new profile the scene's shaders compile for 6.3 s of an 8 s call whose drawing takes 0.8 s (RTX 3070, D3D11), so the profile's `GPUCache` is kept in the temporary folder (`space-film-shaders`, one for Electron and one for Chrome) and laid into each new profile; a preview is then a call of 2.2 s and a check one of 1.4 s. Nothing else of a profile reaches the next browser. A browser is asked to leave before it is killed, so it writes down what it compiled, and one that compiled a program the kept folder lacks (a changed shader, the traced disc's pass) leaves its folder in the kept one's place. Kept programs draw the picture new ones draw, byte for byte. Twelve previews started at once ended in 18 s; with each compiling its own and holding the turn through its browser's start they took 102 s.

## Narration

A narration is made outside the page, by `scripts/voice.mjs`; the `film-voice` skill is the how-to, the tags before each line included.

- **A film's `films/<film>_voice.mjs`** names what is said and the voices that say it, and calls `voiceFilm`, whose jobs are `speak`, `verify` and `mix`. A take is named by what made it (profile, seed, engine, its fields, the line) and kept in `films/voice/`, out of git, so only a changed line is spoken again. The clips read each line's length and the second of each cue word as their `input` and mark where a line starts (`say:`); `mix` lays the takes at those marks. A reel's lines and cues can be read from its clips themselves (`linesOfReel`), so a line is written once, in the clip that says it.
- **A voice is spoken by VoiceStudio on this machine** (one client at a time: the `voicestudio-api` skill) or, with `servers`, by audio.cpp servers on a rented machine, for a model this card cannot hold; a take needs no machine once spoken, so the machine lives for the speaking alone and is deleted after it. `scripts/drama3_server.sh` starts such a machine for Fish Audio Drama 3: on one A100 80 GB at Runpod it held two servers and spoke the 60 lines of `fourth_dimension_pop` in 3 min 22 s, 0.42 s of work for a second of speech; the prebuilt Linux CUDA server of audio.cpp is built for compute capability 7.5 to 9.0, so not for Blackwell cards. `scripts/drama3_pod.mjs` rents such a machine, speaks a voice's missing lines on it and deletes it in one command. Drama 3's licence is non-commercial.

## Verify

A film change is done when each check that applies passes:

- Two previews with one seed are the same bytes, and a rendered mp4 plays (`<video>` in Chrome, or mediabunny's `Input` in Node for its tracks).
- A change to the encoders or to what feeds them (`encodeVideo`, `encodeLocally`, `frame_pack.ts`, `mcp/local_*.cjs`): a film renders through both, with `SPACE_BROWSER_ENCODER=1` and without, every frame is there, and a frame taken from each file is the still of its second and not the frame beside it (38 dB and more against the still at 1080p, about 31 for a neighbour).
- A change to reels: a reel of one clip previews to the film's own bytes.
- A change to what fractal films share (`film_render.ts`, `readPace`, `readCaptions`, `readShots`, the captions, the caption text, `playScript`): a particle film and a fractal film still render.
