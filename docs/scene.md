# The scene's subsystems

The laws each part of the scene keeps, beyond the invariants of `CLAUDE.md` ("Render performance"), which every change keeps. Read the section of the part a change touches; a new thing drawn round the holes, on the sheet or in 3D follows the section it joins.

## The particles' laws

- **Particles keep what speed Inertia lets them.** A push of the holes (pull, Orbital Force, the spin's drag, the second hole) moves a particle at once and, while Inertia is above 0, also adds to its sideways speed (`vx`, `vy`), which carries it on at that share per 60 Hz frame; a new force on the particles is such a push.
- **One law of gravity for the field and the stars.** The pull is the Paczynski-Wiita potential the orbiting stars move in, Newton's times (r / (r - r_s))^2 by Relativity (`wellPull`), and the field's speed of light follows from its own gravity, c^2 = 2 GM / r_s (`lightSpeed`), so a place in Schwarzschild radii and a speed in the speed of light mean the same to the stars and to the field. A particle's speed share says what it is: above 0 it flies with the field; 0 is matter born in orbit (`orbitBirth`), below 0 the debris of a torn star (`burstBirth`). Either keeps all its speed, feels no Orbital Force and no Gravity Reach, loses angular momentum to Viscosity round the nearer hole, and is made anew past Orbit Escape; which particle is matter in orbit is its own number against Orbit Birth (`ownOrbit`), so a change of the share moves particles between the two at once. A torn star is one frame's numbers (`burst`..`burstSeed`), which the step reads to set a share of the field, chosen by the tearing's own number, where the star came apart. The holes' own speeds (`holeVelX`..`companionVelZ`) come from where they stood at the last step.
- **A particle's light is shifted by its own motion.** With Doppler above 0 the step works out the Doppler factor of the speed a particle kept, seen through this frame's pose (`dopplerShift`), lights it by its power Beaming and writes the doublings times Doppler Tint into the stroke (the fourth number of `v_style`), which the stroke shader turns toward the ring's two side colours. A particle that keeps no speed (Inertia 0, the flight) is not shifted, and costs nothing beyond the gate.
- **3D is a second step, not a branch in the first.** `stepSpace` (`src/lib/field_step.ts`) steps the same field with the laws of `stepFlat` in space: the holes are points (`holeWorldX`..`companionWorldZ`), gravity follows the true distance and ends at the sphere of Gravity Reach, the horizon is a sphere (`respawnBeyondSphere`), the spin's drag turns space about the axis as it stands in the scene, Orbital Force swirls about an axis `advance` hands over (`swirlX`..`swirlZ`: the line of flight, turned to the spin's axis by Swirl Follows Spin), the opening reveals a sphere round the blast's place (`fromBlast`), and Inertia keeps speed along all three axes (`vz` as well). The shader picks one by `threeD`, so a new law of the particles goes into both. Straight on it draws what the flat step draws.
- **The camera in 3D.** Each particle is seen through two poses of the camera (`cameraPose` in `src/lib/camera_pose.ts`: its circle round the hole, then its move, its head and its roll): the frame before's where its stroke starts, this frame's where it ends, and a turn of more than `cameraCutTurn` in a frame, or a move past `cameraCutShift`, starts the trails afresh. Its brightness is judged by its own move, both ends seen through this frame's pose (`own`), so a camera that turns streaks the trails and lights nothing. The circle places things in the scene; only the pose sees them.

## 3D: the rest of the scene

The holes, the pair's centre, the blast and a swallowed star's flare are places of the scene: `advance` projects each (`look`), draws the holes at their apparent size (`holeScale`), and whatever is laid on the screen reads where they are seen.

- Two holes at different depths are seen through each other (`u_pairDepth`, `companionBend`): the nearer bends the light of the farther, and its shadow hides the farther one's ring.
- Spin Tilt and Spin Axis are seen straight on, so in 3D the axis stands in the scene: the shadow, the lens and the ring see it turned by the camera, the particles and the orbiting stars feel it as it stands.
- The orbiting stars and the flows are places of the scene that their vertex shaders turn with the camera's three columns (`u_turnX`, `u_turnY`, `u_turnZ`).
- The sky is read where the bent ray points, through the camera's turn (`u_turnRight`, `u_turnDown`, `u_turnAhead`, no turn in the flat mode): the stars and the nebula on a sphere round the camera (`sphereStar`, `nebulaSphere`), even in every direction, the Lensed Grid on the faces of a box (`skyFace`). The flat mode's backdrop turns into the sphere by the share the camera has eased in (`u_skySphere`), both worked out only between 0 and 1. The tint of the particles takes the line of flight from the same turn (`u_flight`).
- The sheet is a surface of the scene, the main hole's equator (`equator` in `advance`) lifted along the way it faces by the depth of the well, so the well's bottom stands at the hole, seen through the camera's own pose as the particles are (`laySpaceSheet`): from the side, from below, near and moving, with nothing held back.

## What stands in front of the holes

- **Sides.** In 3D `advance` hands the step the nearer hole's distance from the camera (`frontAway`, 0 in the flat mode, whose law takes whatever covers the hole at any depth, with no hole and with a hole behind the camera), and each end of a stroke takes its side by its own depth: a stroke whose start is nearer than that writes its opacity below 0, and one whose ends are on two sides names where along it it crosses, which the stroke fragment shader cuts it at (`stroke` in `field_step.ts`, `v_cut`). Something between two holes is behind the nearer alone, and is the lens's only where that hole's lens reaches it on the picture (`frontReach`, `frontLetGo`, each hole's own distance in `holeFront`, `companionFront`), since the layer behind is read through both lenses and would bend the farther hole's own matter by its own lens.
- **The band.** The boundary is a band, not a plane: over Front Depth (`frontDepth`) behind the hole the share seen through the lens grows as a thin lens's bend does, raised to Front Curve, and what is seen within a shadow there, a spinning hole's outline read from its texture, is the lens's (`BEHIND_GLSL`, one text for the step and the line shaders). A particle of the field or a flow goes wholly to one side past a share of its own (`ownShare`), a trace or a lattice line into both layers at their shares, each end at its own. Front Bend and Between Bend draw a share of the band, and of what stands between two holes, once in front instead, where a point mass bent by its share of the nearer lens shows it (`imageOf`, `bentBy`): the step and the line shaders move the ends of a stroke there, so no layer is read again per pixel.
- **One draw for both layers.** The renderer binds the scene texture and the front scene texture side by side (`sceneBoth`) and fades and stamps both in one draw each: the stroke fragment shader writes two targets, a stroke into the one its vertex shader names (`v_front`) and nothing into the other, which the brighter-of-two blend leaves as it was. The orbit, lattice and flow vertex shaders take their side from their own depth (`SIDE_GLSL`, `u_side`) and are drawn once into the two layers of lines (`linesBoth`). A new thing drawn in strokes goes through the same fragment shader and names its side; drawing a buffer once for each side costs the vertex work twice, which was 13% to 28% of the frame rate with the orbiting stars, 200 000 particles or the flows on.
- **The composite.** The pass reads the front layers where the pixel is (`layerPoint(frag)`), not along the bent ray, after the shadows, the lenses and the rings, behind `u_front`. The camera's zoom, jolt, lens fringe, exposure and the film act on them, the holes' absorption and bends do not, and the waves and the matter front slide them by Front Waves (`u_front.w`, what they slid the bent ray by, `waveMoved`); they are laid over the rest as the brighter of two, or added by Front Add (`u_front.z`). When the front ends the front scene texture stays bound and fades with the scene's until it is dark (`frontLeft`), and is read until then. The sheet keeps a piece nearer than the holes uncleared (`v_front`).

## The lens

The Ray Traced lens (`u_trace.x`) is the one effect that works every pixel (`traceSweep`); the main thread only places its camera (`cameraFor` in `src/lib/lens_trace.ts`, redone only when Einstein Radius changes). A spinning hole (`u_spin.x` above 0) takes the Kerr ray (`kerrRay`, or `kerrRayFrom` where the sines and cosines are at hand, in `src/lib/kerr_glsl.ts`; its reference is `src/lib/kerr_trace.ts`), and its shadow and ring follow the outline `updateShadowShape` works out when spin, tilt or the camera change. The Horizon Grid (`u_horizonGrid.x`) follows the rays of the shadow back to the horizon (`kerrHorizonFrom`, three rays a pixel there, for the width of its lines). The GPU's `asin` and `sin` lose near-equal differences, so take such a quantity (the ray's remaining fall) from an identity, never by subtracting.

The White Hole (`u_whiteHole`, its colour `u_whiteColor`) lights the shadow instead of blacking it. The composite's
shadow block multiplies the colour by `besideHole` (0 inside the shadow), and adds the surface's light where
`besideHole` is 0, weighted by the second hole's shadow (`besideCompanion`) and darkened by the horizon grid's
lines (`gridShade`, the same `horizonLines` that paint a black hole's grid, so the lines are lensed the same way).
At 0 the shadow stays black and the grid light is added as it was; the shadow's edge, the lens and the photon ring
do not change. The surface is the shadow itself, so it needs one (`horizon` above 0), and the field's laws are
untouched: a white hole's matter is a flow told to leave (knobs.md, section 4).

## The zoom

The camera's zoom redraws, it does not magnify. A zoom above 1 (the Zoom setting, the intro's camera) is applied to the geometry: the particle and orbit vertex shaders draw at `u_zoom`, and the composite pass reads the layers through `u_layerZoom`, the zoom they were drawn at. Every edge and hairline in that pass is measured in `px`, the scene pixels under one screen pixel, so nothing softens or thickens with the zoom; a new shape there follows the same rule. A zoom below 1 is left to the composite pass. The camera pans by moving the point its zoom leaves in place (`zoomX`, `zoomY` in `advance`), kept inside the frame, with nothing new in any shader; whatever places things on the screen (captions, the sheet, `scene.holeShiftX`) reads that point. A stroke starts where the particle stood in the last frame's picture (`u_zoomFrom`), so a moving camera streaks the trails and a camera cut (a jump past `cameraCutZoom` or `cameraCutShift` in one frame) starts them afresh. The title sprite and the intro's dust still soften when magnified.

## The space grid: Lensed Grid, Sheet, Lattice

- The Lensed Grid is a block of the composite shader gated by `u_skyGrid`, read at the bent ray like the stars.
- The Sheet (`src/lib/space_grid.ts`) is a static buffer of line pieces that the grid vertex shader places, dents (`dent`, whose physics its comment gives) and ripples, in one instanced draw over the finished frame while Sheet is above 0, clearing its lines round each shadow except a piece nearer the camera than the holes; with Sheet Rings above 0 the same buffer is drawn as rings at equal steps of the well's depth and spokes across them (`u_rings`). `placeSheet` lays out the surface and the pose it is seen through, a few numbers a frame: the flat mode's own camera (`layFlatSheet`), the 3D camera's pose (`laySpaceSheet`), blended while 3D eases in; nothing about the sheet is computed per line in JavaScript.
- The exceptions are two. The first is Light Front's arrival-time field (`src/lib/light_front.ts`): a fast march of the eikonal round the hole on the main thread, about 5 ms, run by `layFlash` in the renderer only when the flash's distance from the hole, the sheet's reach or a star's surface changes, and uploaded as a half-float texture the grid vertex shader reads; with Light Front at 0 it never runs. State that must follow every frame of a film, such as where that flash went off, is kept in `advance`, never in the renderer, whose drawing a still or a contact sheet skips on the frames it does not show.
- The flash's photons (Photons, `src/lib/photons.ts`, `src/lib/photon_program.ts`) are the second: every photon's ray through the same medium, traced on the main thread into a table of places by coordinate time (about 0.05 ms a photon), which goes up as a float texture once; each frame the photon program reads every photon at the run's time and draws its trail on the sheet's surface in one instanced draw over the frame, after the sheet. `advance` decides where they are traced from (`followFlash`): never while the light has not run, again only once the flash's place from the hole stands still after it moved, and in between the last trace is drawn moved with the flash, so a hole dragged or 3D easing in does not trace every frame. The renderer only follows what `advance` says. With Photons at 0 the program is not even built.
- The Lattice (`src/lib/space_lattice.ts`) is the same law in three dimensions: a static buffer of line pieces, made when it is first shown, that its vertex shader draws in towards the holes (the same `dent`, one GLSL text for both) and sets before the camera, in one instanced draw into the orbits' layer, which the composite pass reads while `u_orbits` is 1, and a second into the front layer of lines while something can stand in front of the holes; only while Lattice is above 0.

## Particles on the sheet

Drawn there, never moved there. With Particles on Sheet (`gridRide`) above 0 the particles are the sheet's vectors: each stroke lies on its surface and shows where matter, or space, goes on it. A particle within Ride Depth (`gridRideDepth`) of the main hole's equator is dropped onto that plane along the way it faces, and the point of the surface over it is seen through the sheet's pose; the ride share, falling to 0 at the slab's edge, blends the two places in the scene before they are seen (`laidOn`). The step's shader does it in `stroke`, for `stepFlat` and `stepSpace` alike, behind `u_ride.x`, and the flow vertex shader from a flow's place in the scene; the particles' laws know nothing of it.

The sheet's surface and its projection are one GLSL text, `SHEET_GLSL` in `src/lib/webgl_shaders.ts` (`sheetDepth`, `sheetSeen`, `sheetScreen`), which the grid program, the step (`RIDE_GLSL`) and the flows all read, with the numbers `sendSheet` hands each; a change to how the sheet is placed, dented or seen goes there once. The step reads the sheet through `StepSettings.sheet`, so `advance` lays it out (`placeSheet`) before the particles are stepped, drawn or not. Both ends of a stroke are seen through the sheet as it lies now: seen through the sheet of the frame before, a camera that moves would draw every slow particle as a long line.

## The flows

A flow has no state. The flows (`src/lib/flows.ts`) are particles told where to go, three bands round the main hole, each in the hole's equator (`equator` in `advance`), leaned toward the pair's orbit or the camera's plane (`leanPlane`), tipped by its own tilt. Where a particle stands, in its flow's plane and the height above it, is worked out in the flow vertex shader (`placeAt`) from its own four numbers in a static buffer (made anew only when Flow Seed changes) and the flow's clock, and its trail is the stretch of the same path behind it, in pieces, drawn anew into the orbits' layer in one instanced draw for each flow whose brightness is above 0 (and a second into the front layer of lines while something can stand in front of the holes), and only on a frame that is shown. `stepFlow` and `turnFlow`, which hands the shader where the camera sends the flow's three directions (`u_turnX`, `u_turnY`, `u_turnZ`), are the few numbers a frame a flow costs the main thread. Nothing of a flow's particle is kept from one frame to the next or computed in JavaScript, so a new motion of a flow is a line of `placeAt` and a uniform, never a per-particle array. With Particles on Sheet a flow is laid on the sheet in that shader (`RIDE_GLSL`). A flow that ends inside the horizon feeds the ring by `feedRing`, a loop over the ring's bins, gated like the rest. With Particles off (`fieldEnabled`) the field is neither stepped nor drawn.

## The traced disc

Read along the lens's rays, never drawn on the screen. With Traced Disc (`flowTrace`) above 0, the Ray Traced lens on and a hole in front of the camera, the flows round the main hole with no tilt and not on the sheet are drawn by the flow program into a map of the equator seen from straight above it (`drawDiscMap`, the equator's own two directions, out to the farthest of their edges, `DISC_MAP_SIZE` texels a side, mipmapped after each draw) and not into the layers of lines, by the share `flowTrace` takes. The composite pass's `discLight`, gated by `u_disc.x`, follows the pixel's ray to where it crosses the equator (`discRadii` in `src/lib/disc_glsl.ts`, closed form in Jacobi's functions; its double-precision reference is `src/lib/disc_trace.ts`), reads the map there nearest first, each crossing hiding what lies behind it, and shifts the light by the orbit's Doppler factor.

- It reads the map in uniform control flow, every pixel and every crossing alike, so the GPU's level of detail holds; a crossing that is not there is masked after the read.
- The disc uses the lens's camera and pixel-to-ray rule (`u_trace`), so a change to how the Ray Traced lens places its camera or maps a pixel to a ray goes into `discLight` too, and a change to the arithmetic of `discRadii` into `disc_trace.ts`, checked against it in numbers.
- The composite pass with the disc is a program of its own (`compositeDiscFragmentShaderSource`, the same text with `TRACED_DISC` defined), built the first time the disc is traced and used only while it is, so the pass of every other scene carries none of its code: in one program, gated or not, it cost the default scene a third of its frame rate. Off, the flows are drawn as strokes and the pass does none of this.

## A second black hole

Gated. `stepPair` (`src/lib/companion.ts`) is a few numbers per frame, the pair's motion in its own plane; `advance` sets the two in the scene with that plane's directions (`pairPlane` in `scene.ts`, from Orbit Tilt and Tilt Toward), in both modes, and the flat mode draws them as they lie across the screen; the field's step takes the companion's capture and pull in one block behind `s_companionRadius`; its lens, shadow, ring and spiral wave sit behind `u_companion.z` and `u_pairWave.x`, and the sheet takes its second well and wave from uniforms.

The merger is the pair's own motion: `stepPair` gives the merged hole's tone and the pair's last angle, and `advance` turns them into `u_holeOval` and a wave that the intro's wave block draws through `u_gravityWaveCenter`, `u_gravityWaveTurn` and `u_pairFront`; the opening's ringdown and a fired one stand still. The hole a merger leaves spins about the orbit's axis.

Every gravitational wave moves the picture across its path, never along it, weakens as 1 over the distance (`waveReach`), and is seen as the slice through its source that faces the camera (`planeQuad`, which the ringing horizon's oval goes through too, in the plane `u_pairPlane` gives: the pair's orbit while there is one, else the plane across the line of flight); a new wave does all three.

## Orbiting stars

They leave lines, not a picture. `stepOrbiters` (`src/lib/orbiters.ts`) integrates at most `MAX_ORBITERS` stars in the scene's three coordinates, each born in the hole's `equator` tipped about a node of its own and then moved by its motion alone, so a spin axis that turns later precesses its orbit; the spin acts on it as the gravitomagnetic field of a spinning mass about the axis as it stands in the scene, in the sense the field's particles are dragged. It hands the renderer one row a frame (a new one every `STRETCH_SECONDS`, three numbers a point), which goes up in one `bufferSubData` into a ring of rows on the GPU; the orbit program draws the whole layer anew each frame around the hole at the camera's zoom, turned by the camera's three columns (`u_turnX`, `u_turnY`, `u_turnZ`) in 3D and straight on in the flat mode, and the composite pass reads it only while `u_orbits` is 1. How a trace ages (`dimmed`) is told in `orbiters.ts`.

## The bloom

The frame's last pass, and gated. With Bloom (`bloom`) above 0 the composite pass and the sheet draw into the bloom's frame instead of the screen, and `bloom` in the renderer halves its light above the threshold into half-float levels (as many as Bloom Reach asks, at most `BLOOM_LEVELS`), adds them back up and lays the first over the frame on the screen. At 0 the frame goes straight to the screen, with no pass, no texture bound and no change to the composite program. Whatever is drawn over the finished frame goes in before it, so it blooms too.
